title: Using AI effectively
---

Three elements determine the quality of results you get from AI. Getting the right results often means experimenting with all three of these elements.

<img src="/public/ai-elements.png" alt="Diagram showing the three elements of AI — clear requirements, good prompts, capable model" width="450">

## Clear requirements

AI performs best when you know exactly what you want from it. The more specific your requirements, the more consistent and useful the output. Vague instructions produce vague results.

Before starting any AI task, you must understand what good output looks like. The more you know about the output format, the level of detail and the audience it is intended for, the more clarity and consistency you get in your results.

## Good prompts

Prompts need more context than you might expect. AI cannot infer what a human colleague would pick up naturally. It needs explicit instructions, background detail and examples.

Treat prompts as living documents. The best results come from refining and iterating over time. For more complex tasks, a well-crafted prompt can take more effort than writing the output yourself. But once you have a good prompt, it can be reused and the time saving scales.

Detailed, structured prompts with lots of context consistently outperform shorter prompts.

## Capable model

All models are not equal. Two products using the same underlying model can produce different results depending on how the provider has configured it. The free Microsoft Copilot Chat, for example, does not perform with the same quality as the paid Microsoft 365 Copilot, even though they share the same name.

Choose the right model for the task. Advanced reasoning models produce higher quality output but use significantly more energy. If a lighter model can do the job, use it.
