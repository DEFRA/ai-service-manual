# NRF Discovery

A 12-week discovery completed in September 2025. The team used AI throughout and delivered comprehensive results with fewer people in the same timeframe.

## Overview

This discovery ran from July to September 2025 with a reduced team size compared to a standard government discovery project. The team made a deliberate decision to use AI tools extensively across the project, not just in isolated pockets of work. Despite operating with 36.5% fewer people than normal, the team delivered a complete discovery within the standard 12-week timeframe, producing all expected outputs to a high standard. This proved that AI could accelerate delivery work without compromising quality. The team's approach was pragmatic. They experimented with different tools for different tasks, learned what worked and what did not, and adjusted their methods throughout the project.

## How AI supported the work

User research synthesis became significantly faster. AI helped summarise interview transcripts and identify patterns across multiple sessions, cutting synthesis time by 56%.

Deep research into technical and policy domains meant the team could investigate areas outside their immediate expertise without needing additional specialists for every question.

Document analysis sped up dramatically. The team used AI to review existing documentation, extract relevant sections, and identify gaps or inconsistencies.

Prototyping allowed the team to test concepts with users quickly. AI generated working code, letting the team focus on design and user needs rather than technical implementation.

Website scrapers gathered data from public sources, automating work that would otherwise have required manual collection.

Code exploration helped the team understand unfamiliar codebases and technical systems faster.

## Outcomes and impact

### 56% faster user research synthesis

User research completion and synthesis took 56% less time than traditional methods. Insights reached decision-makers faster.

### 36.5% smaller team delivered full outputs

The team operated with 36.5% fewer people while maintaining the same output quality. This efficiency came from AI handling time-consuming tasks like transcription, initial analysis, and document review.

### Better collaboration across stakeholders

AI tools helped create clearer documentation and summaries. Better information sharing led to better alignment between the delivery team and the wider programme.

### Quality matched standard discovery expectations

The discovery received positive feedback at its assessment. The quality of outputs matched or exceeded expectations for a standard discovery, despite the smaller team.

## Challenges and what the team learned

Ethics and bias required constant attention. The team established validation steps to check AI outputs for bias or inaccuracies before using them in decision-making. Explainability presented challenges when AI produced useful outputs without making clear how it reached conclusions. The team learned to assess reasoning carefully rather than accepting outputs at face value.

Technical complexity increased with multiple tools. Maintaining and troubleshooting AI tools added overhead to workflows. The team learned to verify outputs carefully and use AI as a starting point rather than a final answer.

The learning curve was steeper than expected. Team members needed time to learn effective prompt writing and recognise unreliable outputs. This required upfront investment, but as skills improved, efficiency gains accelerated.
